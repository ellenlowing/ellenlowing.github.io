Selected work / mobile AR game

Wireloop

Trace a wire through your room to reveal a hidden shape.

My role

Lead AR Developer · The New York Times

Focus

  • Game concept & prototyping
  • Spatial tracking & gameplay
  • Custom shaders & visual feedback

Inspired by the classic buzzwire game, Wireloop turns your phone into a ring that follows a wire in space. Tracing the path reveals a hidden image through anamorphosis: a shape that comes together from the right viewpoint.

My concept won an internal game pitch. As lead AR developer, I took it from prototype to production, working with designers and artists to bring the series into the visual language of NYT Games.

Released on Instagram

Built in Meta Spark Studio, the series received more than 1.8 million views and 30,000 captures.

01 / Spatial interaction

Move the phone, reveal the shape

The familiar wire-loop toy gave players a simple starting point: guide the ring along the path. In AR, moving the phone moves the ring, encouraging players to explore the curve in physical space.

Under the hood

With no reliable physics library in Spark Studio, I wrote custom tracking logic using the device position, nearest wire position, and dot-product calculations to follow progress along the path.

02 / Visual feedback

A visual kind of buzz

Without the toy’s physical buzz, the ring and wire communicate how well you’re following the path. A custom fragment shader changes their brightness with the distance between them, giving feedback without a score display.

When the ring slips off, the nearest wire segment tapers and crumbles. Vertex displacement and particles turn the mistake into a visible response, keeping the interaction playful and low-pressure.
