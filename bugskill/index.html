BUGSKILL

Catch flies in your own room—with your hands, quick reflexes, and a few unexpected power-ups.

Game Designer and Developer

01 / Hand-tracked interactions

See a fly. Try to catch it.

BUGSKILL starts with something familiar: trying to catch an annoying fly. Players wave, clap, and slap using their hands, without picking up a controller.

Wave, then clap

Waving one hand slows a fly down. Clapping both hands together catches it. Wide Motion Mode helps track these fast movements.

Slap it against a surface

A slap counts when the hand, fly, and room surface collide together—turning the furniture around you into part of the game.

Flies that belong in your room

We used the Scene API and MRUK to generate flight paths and spawn points from the player’s room. Scene volume colliders help flies avoid obstacles, while spatial audio and Doppler effects help players hear where they are and how they’re moving.

02 / Power-ups & systems

More ways to catch a fly

Power-ups give players a choice in how they play. I built an extensible item framework so new items could share the same core behavior without rewriting it for each one.

Froggy

A little help from a frog hand puppet—pinch to extend its tongue 😝

Insecticide spray

Spray once to slow flies down, then again to kill them.

Finger gun

Make a finger gun and flex your thumb to shoot 👉

Finger slingshot

A powerful two-handed weapon: form a slingshot with one hand, then use the other to launch a bomb.

Design note: diegetic UI

I used an hourglass ⏳ to show the time remaining in each round and a potion bottle 🧪 to show progress toward the fly-killing target. These objects keep the round’s status in the game world, so players can check it at a glance.

Under the hood

Items inherit a shared base class with in-store, in-use, and idle states. ScriptableObjects hold item data for the shop, keeping behavior and content easy to extend.

Learn it before the next round

The shop lets players explore their options before buying. Prices are always visible; grabbing a power-up reveals its description and plays a preview video showing how to use it.

A purchase checks the player’s funds and makes the item available for the next round.

03 / Progression

Catch. Earn. Gear up.

Each catch earns money, with different rewards for different methods. Score pop-ups make each catch visible, and a progress display shows how close players are to the round’s cash target.

I used ScriptableObjects for level progression, so targets and level data could be tuned in the Unity editor. Reaching the target earns a star and advances the player to the next round.

Catch flies→Earn money→Choose power-ups→Next round

Faster flies, quicker escapes

As players level up, flies move faster and become more sensitive to nearby movement, making them harder to catch.

Under the hood

A Unity overlap sphere detects player proximity and triggers a dodge to a nearby point.

From prototype to team project

A small idea that grew

I started BUGSKILL as a solo prototype during the XR Foundations and Prototyping Bootcamp. After it gained support online, my mentors selected me as team lead to develop it further with fellow students for the Meta Quest Presence Platform Hackathon.

The project brought together physical gestures, room-aware behavior, and a game loop that gives players a reason to keep chasing one more fly.
